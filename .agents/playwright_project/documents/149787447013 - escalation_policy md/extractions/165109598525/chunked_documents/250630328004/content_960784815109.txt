# Escalation Policy

A ticket is escalated when it is flagged for human review before any automated reply
is sent. Escalation is about the kind of risk in the ticket, not about how loudly it
was reported.

## Always escalate

**Security.** Suspicious logins, credentials posted in a public place, session
hijacking, anything the customer describes as a breach. Escalate even when the
customer says they have already handled it.

**Data exposure.** One customer seeing another customer's data, in any amount and in
any direction. A single stray row is an escalation.

**Billing disputes.** A charge the customer says they did not authorise, or a refund
that has already been refused once. Ordinary refund requests are not disputes.

**Legal risk.** Threats of legal action, regulatory correspondence, and deletion
requests that cite a specific law.

## Do not escalate on urgency alone

A production outage with a known cause and a published workaround is urgent and does
not need human review. Neither does a rate-limit error, however severe, when the
limit is documented and the fix is a plan change. Escalation adds a person to the
loop; urgency adds speed. They are different controls and using one for the other
makes both less useful.

## What escalation actually does

The ticket is held before the first reply, an on-call reviewer reads it within the
hour, and the reviewer either releases the draft, rewrites it, or takes the ticket
over entirely. The customer is not told the ticket was escalated.

## Borderline cases

**A customer says their account was accessed from a country they have never visited.**
Escalate. It is a suspicious login even if the customer adds that they think it was
their VPN.

**A customer asks for a refund because they forgot to cancel.** Do not escalate. This
is an ordinary refund request and billing can decide it. It becomes a dispute only
after billing has refused once and the customer comes back.

**A customer's export contains three rows belonging to another tenant.** Escalate.
Volume does not matter for data exposure.

**A customer mentions their lawyer in passing while complaining about downtime.**
Escalate. Threats of legal action are escalated whether or not they are serious,
because judging seriousness is exactly the thing a human should do.
