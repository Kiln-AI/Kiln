# Support Routing Playbook

This playbook is the reference the triage task is built against. It describes which
team owns which kind of ticket, and how priority is decided.

## Teams and what they own

**Billing** owns invoices, refunds, failed payments, plan upgrades and downgrades,
tax documents, and any question about what a customer was charged. A payment that
fails because a card expired is a billing ticket even though the customer will
describe it as an error.

**Technical support** owns errors, outages, timeouts, rate limits, failed imports,
and anything where the product does not do what the documentation says it does. If
reproducing the problem requires looking at logs, it is technical support.

**Account management** owns workspace ownership transfers, seat changes, SSO
configuration, provisioning, and renewals. These tickets almost always require
verifying who is asking before anything is changed.

**Product feedback** owns feature requests, and complaints about behaviour that is
working as designed. A customer who is unhappy that exports are limited to 10,000
rows is product feedback; a customer whose 500-row export failed is technical
support.

## Choosing between two teams

Route to the team that can close the ticket without a handoff. When two teams could
each close it, prefer the one that owns the system where the change has to be made,
not the one that owns the system the customer noticed the problem in.

## Priority

Urgent means the customer cannot work at all, data is at risk, or money is moving
incorrectly right now. High means a core workflow is broken but a workaround exists.
Medium means something is wrong and inconvenient, but the customer can proceed. Low
covers questions, requests, and anything the customer is not blocked on.

Plan tier does not set priority. An enterprise customer asking a how-to question is
low priority. A free-tier customer whose data has been exposed is urgent.

## Worked examples

**"My invoice shows two charges for March."** Billing, high. The customer is not
blocked from using the product, but money has moved incorrectly and the fix is a
refund only billing can issue. Do not route this to technical support because the
duplicate came from a retry in the payment pipeline; billing owns the outcome.

**"Imports have been failing since this morning with a 502."** Technical support,
urgent if imports are the customer's main workflow and no workaround exists, high if
they can upload manually while it is fixed. Ask which before choosing.

**"Can you move the workspace to my colleague, I am leaving the company."** Account
management, medium. This is an ownership transfer and needs identity verification
before anything changes. It is not urgent unless the departure is today.

**"Exports stop at 10,000 rows and that is useless for us."** Product feedback, low.
The limit is documented and working as designed. Route it to product feedback with a
summary that names the customer's actual use case, because that is the part the
product team cannot get from the ticket title.

**"Someone from another company can see our dashboards."** Technical support, urgent,
and flagged for human review under the escalation policy. Route it and escalate it;
these are two separate decisions and both apply here.

## Writing the summary

The summary is read by whoever picks the ticket up, not by the customer. Name what
broke, what the customer already tried, and what you could not determine from the
ticket. Two sentences is usually enough. Do not restate the ticket title, and do not
speculate about the cause: an incorrect guess in a summary is repeated by the next
three people who read it.

## When you are not sure

Route to the team that owns the system, and say in the summary what you were unsure
about. A ticket in the wrong queue with an honest summary is cheaper to fix than a
ticket in the right queue with a summary that hides the ambiguity.
